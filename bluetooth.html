How this works

Two separate estimates, with very different reliability. Worth knowing which is which.

Distance — rough but real

Every Bluetooth advertisement arrives with an RSSI (received signal strength). Signal falls off predictably with distance, so distance is inverted out of it:

d = 10 ^ ((txPower − RSSI) / (10 × n))

Expect roughly ±30–50% error. Bodies, walls, metal and even phone orientation all move RSSI by 10 dB or more, which is a 2× swing in distance. Treat it as near / mid / far, not a tape measure. The ± range under each reading is the honest bound from live signal variance.

Direction — needs you to turn around

No phone can read bearing off a Bluetooth packet. True direction finding (BLE 5.1 AoA/AoD) needs an antenna array your phone does not expose to a browser.

So this uses body shadowing instead. Your torso blocks 2.4 GHz by 10–15 dB. Turn slowly through a full circle: signal peaks when you are facing the device and dips when your back is to it. RSSI is bucketed by compass heading, and the peak of that circular pattern is the bearing.

  • Hold the phone flat, screen up, out in front of your chest.
  • Turn a slow, full 360° — about 10 seconds.
  • The arrow firms up as the sweep fills in. Confidence is shown live.
  • Walk a few steps and sweep again to confirm.

It resolves direction to maybe ±30° on a good sweep. Enough to tell you which end of the room, not enough to point at a drawer.

What you will not see

Devices already paired and connected to something usually stop advertising, so they go invisible. iPhones, AirTags and modern Android devices rotate their MAC address every ~15 minutes for privacy, so a device may reappear as a stranger. Neither is fixable from a browser.

Browser support

Continuous scanning of all nearby devices needs the Web Bluetooth Scanning API:

  • Android Chrome — works, after enabling chrome://flags/#enable-experimental-web-platform-features, then restarting Chrome.
  • Desktop Chrome / Edge — same flag.
  • iOS Safari — no Web Bluetooth at all, and Apple has not shipped it. A third-party browser such as Bluefy supports it.

Without the flag, the Pick one device fallback still works everywhere Web Bluetooth exists — you choose a device from the system chooser and it gets tracked individually. Demo mode in Settings runs the full radar on simulated devices, so you can see the direction finding work on any browser.
